1. ABOUT THE POST

The Mine-Void Solar Installation Technician (Grade II) is responsible for the installation, commissioning, inspection, and field maintenance of photovoltaic (PV) solar panels deployed across the decommissioned open-cast coal mine sites of Korba district — specifically the Gevra, Dipka, and Kusmunda mine voids — now converted under the Abandoned Mines (Solar Re-Use) Act, 2043.

This role did not exist before 2026. A coal mine in 2026 employed blasting engineers and dragline operators; it did not employ solar PV technicians. The land on which these panels stand was, twenty-five years ago, a working pit. The technician who maintains them walks the same ground.

DutyDescriptionFrequency
Panel installation & racking Assemble and mount monocrystalline PV modules on stabilised overburden benches and compacted mine-floor surfaces. Follow site-specific ground-load tolerances certified under IS 17001:2048 (Mine Terrain Solar Structures). Use mine-grade PPE at all times — ground subsidence risk applies. Daily (on active installation stretches)
Electrical wiring & inverter connection Connect string cables, DC combiner boxes, and AC inverter units. Use certified insulation-resistance testers before energisation. Log all wiring records into the National Solar Asset Registry (NSAR) portal. Daily
Performance monitoring Read inverter dashboards and handheld irradiance meters. Flag any panel generating below 85% of rated output for inspection. Report soiling (coal-dust accumulation on panels is the primary efficiency loss on mine sites). Daily
Panel cleaning & dust management Operate robotic cleaning units on flat-ground arrays; conduct manual cleaning with deionised-water kits on terraced bench arrays. Coal-dust accumulation at Gevra sites reduces output by up to 22% — this duty is designated high-priority. Twice weekly
Ground stability patrol Walk assigned panel zones and inspect for subsidence cracks, slope erosion, or drainage blockage that may compromise racking foundations. Issue a red-flag report if any deformation exceeds 15 mm per NDMA Mine Solar Protocol, 2046. Weekly
Community boundary interface Maintain the mine-edge public exclusion zone. Respond to queries from adjoining gram panchayats on dust, noise from installations, and visual access. Log all interactions. As required

🔄 Clause 7 — Transition Provisions (Ex-Coal Workers)

This clause is the political core of the advertisement. It is the only place where the people who held the old jobs are mentioned.

CLAUSE 7: PROVISIONS FOR EX-COAL LEGACY (ECL) CANDIDATES

7.1 Definition. An "Ex-Coal Legacy" (ECL) candidate is any person who, between 01 January 2025 and 31 December 2042, was employed for a continuous period of not less than two years in coal-related operations — including extraction, overburden removal, mine machinery operation, electrical maintenance, transportation, or ancillary contract labour — at any facility within Korba district under NTPC, CSEB, SECL, or their registered sub-contractors.

7.2 Separate Quota. 8 posts (22.2% of total vacancies) are reserved for ECL candidates. These posts shall not lapse to general merit if unfilled in the first round; a dedicated second round will be conducted for ECL candidates only.

7.3 Age Relaxation. The maximum age limit for ECL candidates is 48 years (as of 01.10.2050). This relaxation specifically acknowledges that coal operations at Gevra, Dipka, and Kusmunda mine sites wound down between 2035 and 2042, and workers retrenched at that time would now be aged 43–55. A standard age ceiling of 35 would exclude the majority of displaced workers this cadre was created to absorb.

7.4 Qualification Relaxation & Fast-Track Training. ECL candidates who do not hold the prescribed solar/electrical qualification may apply provisionally. They will be placed in the Mine-Solar Transition Certificate Programme — a 6-month residential course at the repurposed SECL Training Centre, Gevra (now CSERC Solar Skills Centre, Gevra) — fully funded by the authority, with a monthly stipend of ₹ 14,000 during training. Prior experience operating electrical systems in coal mines (HT panels, conveyor drives, pump sets) is credited as 50% of the practical skill test, under 7.6.

7.5 Fee Waiver. ECL candidates are exempt from the ₹ 350 application fee in its entirety.

7.6 Prior Mine-Electrical Experience Credit. Verified experience in mine electrical maintenance, HT/LT panel operation, or mine machinery electrical systems will be counted as equivalent to 50% of the prescribed qualification for the Practical Skill Test component only. Coal dust management experience is additionally credited for the panel-cleaning practical module.

ECL status must be verified by the District Labour Office, Korba. Acceptable evidence: EPFO records, SECL / NTPC service book, or affidavits from registered trade unions (INTUC, AITUC, BMS) corroborated by the District Collector. Incomplete or unverifiable claims will render the application void without refund.

What this clause does — and does not — do

✅
It acknowledges the gap in time

The age relaxation to 48 is a direct admission that the 8–15 year window between coal job loss and this recruitment is too long for older workers to have naturally requalified on their own resources.

✅
Mine electrical skills transfer

Unlike ash reclamation, solar PV work genuinely maps onto skills coal workers held — HT/LT panels, cable management, machinery electrical systems. The experience credit in 7.6 acknowledges this rather than treating ex-miners as blank slates.

✅
Training on the same ground

The Fast-Track programme runs at the old SECL Training Centre, Gevra — a building ex-miners already know. This is either a thoughtful continuity or a hollow gesture, depending on what you think matters.

⚠️
Contract labour proof remains hard

Many Korba coal-era workers were sub-contracted through agencies. EPFO records are incomplete, misattributed, or absent. The trade union affidavit route is the only realistic path for thousands — and it depends on unions still being organised in 2050.

❌
8 posts for 18,000 displaced workers

At peak, Korba's coal complex employed ~18,000 direct workers. Eight ECL posts is symbolic at a scale that does not merit the word "transition". It is a clause. It is not a policy.

❌
No provision for widows or dependants

Workers who died of respiratory disease, silicosis, or accident between 2026 and 2050 are not in this advertisement. Their families are not mentioned. The absence is the loudest thing on this page.

Timeline: Korba's Coal-to-Solar Journey (2026–2050)

2026

Korba produces ~497.9 lakh tonnes fly ash (FY 2024-25). NTPC Korba Super Thermal at full capacity. Gevra is India's largest open-cast coal mine. Zero solar panels on mine land.

2030

Coal India production begins structural decline. First NTPC unit in Korba decommissioned. ITI Korba launches Solar PV Technician course (NCVT). 4,400 mine workers non-renewed.

2037

Dipka mine pit extraction ends. SECL surveys 6,200 acres of Dipka void for solar feasibility. CIMFR publishes ground-stability assessment. First pilot array (50 MW) installed on stabilised benches. 10,000 coal jobs gone.

2043

Abandoned Mines (Solar Re-Use) Act enacted. CSERC Mine-Void Solar Authority constituted. SECL Training Centre, Gevra, repurposed as CSERC Solar Skills Centre. Last underground coal extraction in Korba ceases.

2047

Gevra mine-void solar park reaches 2 GW. Korba's electricity is now exported as clean power rather than coal. Pollution index falls from 165 to 142. Unemployment in ex-coal worker households: 31%.

2050

This advertisement. 36 posts on the land that 18,000 people worked. 8 reserved for those workers. The solar panels stand on the same ground the draglines moved. The technicians who maintain them may not be the same people who moved it.

A Note on What This Advertisement Cannot Say

"A real advertisement contains a pay scale, an age limit, a reservation table and an application fee. Those four elements will tell a reader more about 2050 than any slogan."

This advertisement tells you: the solar job pays more than the coal job did, in real terms. The qualification is obtainable in Korba — the ITI course has existed since 2039. The ECL clause names the displaced workers and gives them 8 posts out of 36.

What the advertisement cannot say: that 8 posts for 18,000 displaced workers is 0.04%. That the solar panels stand on ground those workers knew better than anyone — the exact gradient of the Gevra benches, the drainage patterns after rain, where the ground is soft. That knowledge is in the ECL experience credit (Clause 7.6). It is credited at 50%, for one sub-section of one skill test.

The recruitment advertisement is a mirror. The reservation table has two rows where there used to be six. Read the numbers. Count the posts. That is where the transition lives — not in the slogan, but in the arithmetic.
